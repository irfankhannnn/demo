# 03 — RealEstateFlow Language Strategy

> **The language operating system for all Instagram (and adjacent) content targeting real estate brokers in Mumbai & Pune.** Everything the rest of the Content OS produces — hooks, scripts, captions, subtitles, carousels — gets tagged against the vocabulary defined here. Brand baseline is Hinglish (70% English + 30% romanized Hindi, per `.brand/brand-kit.md`). This file extends that baseline for a **two-city, three-language** reality: Mumbai (cosmopolitan Hindi-English) and Pune (strong Marathi pride).

**Inputs:** `.brand/brand-kit.md`, `01-business-memory.md`, `research/trust-signals-hinglish-guide.md`
**Owner:** brand-strategist + seo-content-writer
**Status:** Source of truth for language tagging. Do not contradict.

---

## 1. The Core Decision — Why language is a strategic choice here

The brand kit says "Hinglish." That is correct for a *national* brand voice. But Instagram content is consumed **locally**, with **sound off**, by people whose **identity** is tied to their city's language. Two facts force a more nuanced mix:

1. **Pune is a Marathi-pride market.** Marathi is not just "understood" in Pune — using it signals *"yeh banda apna hai"* (this person is one of us). For local-service B2B (which real estate broking is), in-group language is a trust shortcut. Ignoring Marathi in Pune content is leaving trust on the table.
2. **Mumbai is a melting pot.** Bambaiyya Hindi-English is the lingua franca of the hustle. Marathi is native to Mumbai too (it is Maharashtra's capital), but the *working language of the deal* — site visits, builder calls, client WhatsApp — skews Hindi-English. Pure Marathi in Mumbai can read as regional/political rather than professional.

So the answer is **not** one mix. It is a **base mix + city overlay + segment overlay + content-type overlay**. The sections below define each layer precisely.

> **Mental model:** *Hinglish is the spine. Marathi is the handshake. English is the proof.*

## 2. Recommended Overall Content Language Split

This is the **portfolio-level** target across all RealEstateFlow Instagram output (blend of Mumbai + Pune, all segments, all formats). Individual posts will deviate — that is expected. This is the aggregate you should hit over a month.

| Language band | Target % of content | Role it plays |
|---|---|---|
| **Hindi / Hinglish** (hi-dominant + mixed) | **55%** | The default workhorse. Pan-Maharashtra, both cities, all segments. Carries pain, humour, hustle, CTA. |
| **Marathi-led** (mr-dominant + mr-heavy mixed) | **30%** | The trust + relatability play, heavily weighted to Pune and to local/community content. |
| **English-leaning** (en-leaning) | **15%** | Credibility, data, tech, founder/authority, LinkedIn cross-posts, decision-makers. |

### Reasoning per language

**Hindi / Hinglish — 55% (the spine).**
- It is the **only language that works in both cities at once.** A single Hinglish reel can run in Mumbai and Pune without re-shooting. That production efficiency alone justifies the plurality share.
- It matches the brand kit's existing 70/30 voice, so we stay on-brand and reuse the existing phrase bank.
- It is the natural language of the **deal itself** — "site visit", "booking", "token", "channel partner", "follow-up" are already Hinglish in brokers' mouths. The product (a CRM) lives in this register.
- Brokers are mobile-first hustlers; Hinglish *is* the WhatsApp/Insta-reel register of that hustle. It does not feel like marketing.

**Marathi-led — 30% (the handshake).**
- Pune disproportionately rewards Marathi. Roughly half our Maharashtra TAM intent sits in/around Pune (Pune + PCMC + satellite), and in that market Marathi converts trust faster than any other lever.
- Marathi unlocks **local/community/relatability** content even in Mumbai (drama skits, "tumhi pan asech karta ka?" relatability, festival/Gudi Padwa moments).
- 30% is deliberately **higher than population-share math** would suggest, because Marathi content punches above its weight on *saves, shares, and comments* (in-group pride → engagement → algorithm reach). It is our differentiation vs national CRM brands that only speak Hinglish.
- Capped at 30% (not higher) because Marathi-only content does not travel to Mumbai's cosmopolitan, multi-origin broker base and limits reusability.

**English-leaning — 15% (the proof).**
- Tech terms, ROI/data, and credibility claims land harder in English (the brand kit already mandates English for tech terms).
- Agency Owners and Managers — the people who **pay** — skew more English-comfortable and more LinkedIn-present. The buying decision often needs an English "this is a serious, modern product" signal.
- Kept low (15%) because all-English loses the Indian, in-the-trenches feel and reads as a foreign/enterprise SaaS — exactly what the brand kit says to avoid.

## 3. By-City Breakdown — Mumbai vs Pune (they differ)

Same product, two different language centres of gravity.

| Language band | **Mumbai** | **Pune** | Why the difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hindi / Hinglish | **65%** | **45%** | Mumbai's working language is Bambaiyya Hindi-English; Pune defaults to Marathi in-group. |
| Marathi-led | **18%** | **45%** | Pune rewards Marathi for trust/pride; Mumbai uses Marathi for *flavour*, not as base. |
| English-leaning | **17%** | **10%** | More MNC/finance-adjacent buyers and LinkedIn cross-post in Mumbai. |

### Mumbai notes
- **Default register: Bambaiyya Hindi-English.** Drop a Mumbai-ism occasionally ("apun", "scene", "bole toh", "ekdum", "bindaas") — sparingly, never forced.
- **Marathi as seasoning, not the base.** A Marathi punchline inside a Hinglish reel ("...ani deal gela!") signals local roots without alienating the non-Marathi half of Mumbai (Gujarati, North Indian, South Indian brokers are a large share).
- **Avoid heavy Marathi-only** content for Mumbai geo-targeted sets — it can read as politically coded rather than professional.

### Pune notes
- **Lead with Marathi for relatability, hooks, and drama.** "Tumhi pan Excel madhe CRM chalvताy ka?" (You too running a CRM in Excel?) will out-engage the Hindi version in Pune.
- **Switch to Hinglish/English for the product mechanics and CTA** — even Marathi-proud Punekars say "site visit", "booking", "follow-up", "CRM", "demo" in English. Forcing Marathi onto tech terms reads unnatural.
- **Marathi pride ≠ Marathi everything.** Pune brokers are educated and English-fluent; the win is *opening* in Marathi (the handshake) then doing business in Hinglish/English.
- **Devanagari is more acceptable on-screen in Pune** than in Mumbai (see §6).

## 4. By-Segment Breakdown — language differs by persona

Personas mapped from `01-business-memory.md §2`. The more senior / more the buyer, the more English-comfort; the younger / more field-based, the more Marathi + Hinglish relatability.

| Persona | hi/Hinglish | Marathi-led | English-leaning | Voice posture |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Agency Owner / Brokerage Boss** (ADMIN, the buyer) | 45% | 25% | 30% | Respect + ROI. "Aapki agency", control, hisaab, growth. More English proof (data, RERA, analytics). Use **aap**, never **tu**. |
| **Sales Manager / Team Leader** (elevated MEMBER) | 55% | 25% | 20% | Operational empathy. Team visibility, lead assignment, "team ko kaise track karein". Hinglish workhorse. **aap/tum**. |
| **Young Broker / Consultant** (MEMBER, field hustler) | 55% | 35% | 10% | Peer energy, humour, hustle, FOMO. Drama/skits land here. Heaviest Marathi (esp. Pune). **tu/tum**, "bhai/dada". |
| **New Joiner** (new MEMBER) | 50% | 35% | 15% | Encouraging, simple, "ghabrao mat". Onboarding ease, "pehla din". Marathi welcome for warmth; English only for the app's own labels. **tum**. |

### Honorific rules (critical, cuts across all)
- **Owners & older audiences:** always **aap** (Hindi) / **tumhi / aapan** (Marathi, respectful). Using **tu** to a boss is an instant trust-killer.
- **Young brokers / peers:** **tu / tum** (Hindi), **tu** (Marathi) is fine and builds peer intimacy.
- **When unsure, default up** (more respectful). Easier to recover from "too polite" than "too familiar".

## 6. Subtitle & On-Screen Text Rules (sound-off viewing)

Brokers scroll reels with **sound off**, one-handed, between site visits. Subtitles are not optional — they are the message.

**Core rules**
1. **Every reel gets burned-in subtitles.** No exceptions. Assume audio is muted.
2. **Subtitle language = spoken language.** If she speaks Marathi, subtitle in Marathi (romanized). If Hinglish, subtitle Hinglish. Do **not** translate spoken Marathi into Hindi subtitles — it breaks the in-group trust.
3. **Romanize by default.** "Tumhi pan asech karta ka?" not "तुम्ही पण असंच करता का?". Romanized reads faster on a small screen and matches how brokers type on WhatsApp.
4. **Devanagari (Marathi script) is OK when:** (a) the post is Pune-geo-targeted **and** the line is a short, punchy headline/hook (≤6 words), (b) a festival/cultural greeting (गुढी पाडव्याच्या शुभेच्छा), or (c) a respectful proverb/म्हण used as a card. Never use Devanagari for full-paragraph subtitles — too slow to read in-feed.
5. **Hindi Devanagari: avoid** in subtitles. Romanized Hindi (Hinglish) only, per brand kit ("aapka" NOT "आपका"). Reserve Devanagari for Marathi cultural moments only.
6. **English stays English on screen** — tech terms (CRM, lead, dashboard), numbers (₹2,999, 40% leads), and the product name (RealtyFlow) are always Latin script. Never transliterate "CRM" into देवनागरी or Marathi.
7. **Hook text (first frame):** 3–6 words max, biggest font, can be Marathi for Pune / Hinglish for Mumbai. This is the scroll-stopper; pick the tongue of the geo.
8. **CTA text:** always Hinglish or English ("Free Trial Shuru Karo →", "Link in bio"), pan-city, never Marathi-only (CTA must work everywhere).
9. **One script per word.** Never mix Latin + Devanagari inside a single word (no "Reraचा"). Switch only at word boundaries.
10. **Numbers always Indian:** ₹, lakh, crore. Never $ / million (brand rule).

**On-screen vs spoken example (Pune drama reel)**
- Spoken (Marathi): *"Arre, lead la phone karायचा rahila... ani client ne dusryाकडून ghar ghetla!"*
- Subtitle (romanized Marathi): *"Arre, lead la phone karaycha rahila... ani client ne dusryakadun ghar ghetla!"*
- Hook card (Devanagari OK, short): **लीड हातातून गेली?**
- CTA card (Hinglish): **Follow-up automatic karo → RealtyFlow**

## 8. WHEN TO USE WHICH LANGUAGE — explicit rules + example lines

### 8a. WHEN TO USE MARATHI (`mr-dominant`)
**Use Marathi when:**
- The set is **Pune / PCMC geo-targeted**.
- The content is **emotional, relatable, or community** (drama, "this happens to all of us", festivals, local pride).
- You want the **handshake** — opening a reel/founder video to signal "apna banda".
- The **creator/broker genuinely speaks Marathi** (UGC authenticity).
- A **proverb / म्हण** makes the point sharper.

**Do NOT use Marathi for:** product tech terms, app UI labels, the universal CTA, or Mumbai-wide sets where the audience is multi-origin.

**Example lines**
- Hook (Pune drama): *"Tumhi pan lead WhatsApp madhe shodhta? Mag ji deal gela samjha."* (You also searching leads in WhatsApp? Then consider that deal gone.)
- Relatability: *"Saglyानna vatta CRM mhanje Excel. Pan Excel tumcha follow-up athvun det nahi."* (Everyone thinks CRM means Excel. But Excel won't remind you of your follow-up.)
- Pride/local: *"Pune cha broker, ata Pune chi technology — RealtyFlow."*
- Festival: **गुढी पाडव्याच्या हार्दिक शुभेच्छा!** Navin varshat, navin deals. (Devanagari OK — short, cultural.)

### 8b. WHEN TO USE HINDI / HINGLISH (`hi-dominant`)
**Use Hinglish when:**
- You want **one asset to run in both Mumbai and Pune** (default for efficiency).
- The content is **educational, hustle-energy, CTA-driven, or product-benefit**.
- Targeting **Managers and young brokers** across Maharashtra.
- It's the **caption** under almost any reel (captions skew Hinglish for reach).

**Rules (from brand kit):** romanized Hindi only ("aapka" not "आपका"); English for tech terms; Hindi for emotion ("tension mat lo"); fillers "yaar/bhai/dekho/bas"; ₹/lakh/crore.

**Example lines**
- Pain: *"WhatsApp mein leads dhundh rahe ho? Bhai, ek bhi miss hua toh ₹2 lakh ka commission gaya."*
- Benefit: *"Sab ek jagah — leads, follow-ups, properties, team. Automatic hai bhai."*
- CTA: *"Free trial shuru karo — no credit card. Link bio mein."*
- Hustle: *"Din mein 30 calls, raat ko Excel — yeh kaam ka tareeka nahi, yaar."*

### 8c. WHEN TO USE ENGLISH (`en-leaning`)
**Use English-leaning when:**
- The point is **data, ROI, RERA/legal credibility, or market authority**.
- Talking to **Agency Owners as buyers** (the "this is a serious, modern product" signal).
- **LinkedIn** cross-posts and B2B/partner content.
- Stating **tech features, integrations, security/RBAC** precisely.

**Rules:** stay warm — drop one Hinglish/Marathi line so it doesn't read foreign; keep numbers Indian (₹, crore); never full-corporate-jargon (brand voice forbids it).

**Example lines**
- Authority: *"Maharashtra RERA just changed agent-registration rules. Here's what every brokerage must do — in 60 seconds."*
- Data: *"Indian agencies lose 30–40% of leads to missed follow-ups. That's lakhs in commission every month."*
- Buyer proof: *"Role-based access, commission khata, AI calling, analytics — one platform. Built for Indian brokerages. Dekho demo."*
- LinkedIn: *"How a 15-agent Pune brokerage cut lead leakage to near-zero with CRM automation."*

### 8d. WHEN TO USE MIXED / code-switching (`mixed`)
**Use mixed when:**
- Going for **maximum authenticity** — real UGC, two-character skits, street/Mumbai energy.
- A **Pune** asset that opens Marathi (handshake) but does the product mechanics in Hinglish/English (mr-heavy mixed).
- A **Mumbai** asset with Bambaiyya Hindi-English and a stray Marathi punchline (hi-heavy mixed).

**Rules:** switch only at **word/phrase boundaries** (never inside a word); keep all-Latin in subtitles; let it sound like a real conversation, not a translation exercise.

**Example lines**
- mr-heavy mixed (Pune): *"Arre dada, site visit zhala, client ne booking pan केली — pan token chा follow-up rahila, ani deal cancel! RealtyFlow asta tar reminder आला asta."*
- hi-heavy mixed (Mumbai): *"Apun ko laga deal pakki hai, par client ne bola 'thoda time do' — ab us lead ka follow-up kaun karega? System karega, boss."*
- Founder open (mixed): *"Namaskar! Main [founder] — 200+ brokerages ke saath kaam kiya, aur ek cheez samajh aayi: deal lead se nahi, follow-up se banti hai."*

## 10. Common MISTAKES to Avoid

| Mistake | Why it kills trust | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| **Shuddh / over-formal Hindi** (शुद्ध साहित्यिक हिंदी — "आपका अनुसरण", "गृह संपत्ति") | Sounds like a govt notice or news anchor, not a broker. Brokers never talk like this. | Casual romanized Hinglish: "aapki property", "follow-up". |
| **Wrong/Google-translate Marathi** | Punekars instantly spot machine Marathi or Hindi-grammar-with-Marathi-words. Worse than no Marathi. | Have a native Marathi speaker review every `mr-dominant` asset. When unsure, use Hinglish. |
| **Translating tech terms / UI** ("लीड", "अनुसरण" for follow-up, "क्लाउड CRM" in Marathi) | Brokers say "lead", "CRM", "follow-up" in English. Translation reads alien. | Keep CRM, lead, follow-up, site visit, booking, dashboard in English always. |
| **Forced slang** (stuffing "apun/bindaas/bhidu" into every line, or fake Punekar slang) | Reads as an outsider cosplaying. Cringe = scroll. | One natural slang touch per asset, max. Match the real creator's voice. |
| **Devanagari for Hindi on screen** ("आपका") | Violates brand kit; slower to read; off-voice. | Romanize Hindi ("aapka"). Devanagari reserved for short Marathi cultural lines only. |
| **Pure Marathi in Mumbai-wide sets** | Reads as regional/political; alienates Mumbai's Gujarati/North/South-Indian brokers. | Marathi as accent/punchline in Mumbai; full Marathi only for Pune geo. |
| **Pure English everywhere** | Loses the in-the-trenches Indian feel; reads as foreign enterprise SaaS. | Reserve `en-leaning` for data/authority/LinkedIn; warm it with one Hinglish line. |
| **Using "tu" to Agency Owners** | Disrespectful to the buyer; instant credibility loss. | "aap" (Hindi) / "tumhi / aapan" (Marathi) for owners & elders. |
| **Mixing scripts inside one word** ("Reraचा", "leadला") | Looks broken, hard to read, unprofessional. | Switch only at word boundaries: "RERA cha", "lead la". |
| **$ / million in money** | Off-brand; not how Indians think about money. | ₹, lakh, crore — always. |
| **Subtitling spoken Marathi into Hindi** | Breaks the in-group trust the Marathi audio just built. | Subtitle in the same language that is spoken. |
| **Mismatched honorific within one script** (start "aap", slip to "tu") | Feels inconsistent / careless. | Lock the honorific to the persona before writing. |

## 11. Quick-Reference Cheat Sheet (pin this)

- **Spine = Hinglish (55%), Handshake = Marathi (30%), Proof = English (15%).**
- **Mumbai:** Hinglish base, Marathi as flavour. **Pune:** Marathi handshake, Hinglish/English business.
- **Owners → aap + more English. Young brokers → tu/dada + more Marathi.**
- **Drama/UGC/memes → local tongue. Demo/authority/data → Hinglish/English.**
- **Every reel: burned-in subtitles, romanized, subtitle-language = spoken-language.**
- **Tech terms, UI, CTA, money: always English/Latin (₹, lakh, crore).**
- **Devanagari: short Marathi hooks & festivals only — never Hindi, never paragraphs.**
- **Tag every asset: `hi-dominant` / `mr-dominant` / `mixed` / `en-leaning`.**
- **When unsure: default to Hinglish + more respectful honorific. Native-check all Marathi.**
