## 1. The ICP in one paragraph

Owner-operators of small Indian real estate broking agencies — **Mumbai only for M1** — running 1 to roughly 10 agents, whose business currently lives inside WhatsApp. They are phone-first, time-starved, sceptical of SaaS, and their money pain is a lead that went cold because nobody followed up. The buyer is the **agency owner**; the daily user is the **agent**; the quiet third party is the **sales manager** who has to look competent to both.

## 2. The attention thesis

> **Brokers don't scroll to learn — they scroll to feel seen, to feel ahead of rivals, and to feel safe about money.**

Attention is granted to content that says *"I know your exact Tuesday-afternoon pain"*, and denied to content that says *"transform your workflow."* Every rule in this file is a corollary of that sentence.

## 4. Instagram — the engine

Roughly 60% of broker content consumption (desk-research estimate). Sound off, one-handed, between site visits.

### 4.1 What stops the thumb

| Stop trigger | Why it works |
|---|---|
| A **question that makes them compute their own ₹ number** ("pichle mahine kitne leads aaye? Unme se kitno ka teesra follow-up hua?") | Money equals survival, and a number they produce is one they believe |
| **POV of their exact pain** ("POV: client teesri baar pooch raha hai 'koi update?'") | Recognition reflex — it reads as a mirror |
| **WhatsApp-chaos visual** (200 unread, frantic scroll) | The number-one daily pain, legible with sound off |
| **Real product screen** (the actual CRM, the actual WhatsApp inbox) | The only proof we honestly have, and it is stronger than a statistic |
| **Open loop / contrarian claim** ("CRM matlab Excel nahi") | Curiosity gap they have to close |
| **Recognisable office archetype** (the fumbling new joiner, the lead slipping away) | Casting familiarity means instant comprehension |

> The old draft listed "a ₹ number in the first frame (₹5L gaya)" as the top stop trigger. That is an asserted loss figure and is banned — the replacement above keeps the mechanism and drops the claim.

### 4.2 What gets ignored

Logo or brand intro in the first 2 seconds · "transform your business / digital revolution" · stock photos, foreign offices, suited westerners · a talking head with no on-screen hook text (sound-off means no message) · slow builds over 3 minutes in feed · jargon ("lead qualification pipeline", "omnichannel") that makes them feel stupid.

### 4.3 What earns save, share, comment

| Action | What earns it |
|---|---|
| **Save** | Usable tips, mistake lists, math *they* do, templates |
| **Share (WhatsApp forward)** | Funny, relatable validation of frustration; "tag a broker who…" |
| **Comment** | Contrarian or debate bait, "agree?" prompts, keyword triggers |

### 4.4 What causes a DM or a demo request

| Trigger | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| "DM 'DEMO'" / "comment 'SYSTEM'" keyword loop after a pain reel | Low friction, stays in-app |
| A **real product demo clip** — the AI Employee handling a sample lead end to end | Curiosity strong enough to ask "how?" |
| A genuinely useful answer in the comments | Value first, never pitch first |

**Removed and not to be reinstated:** "peer testimonial from a *named* local agency" (we have no customers — `claims-and-proof-policy.md` §2) and the ROI-calculator lead magnet (the calculator is a placeholder, `product-truth.md` §4).

## 5. WhatsApp — the forwarding layer

100% reach, 0% native publishing. It is not a posting platform; it is where Instagram content goes to spread and where a DM becomes a demo. Attention here is relationship-gated, not algorithm-gated.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What gets attention | A forward from a trusted peer ("dekh ye, apne kaam ka hai"); a reply to *their own* message; a voice note from a real person |
| What gets ignored | Cold broadcast blasts, link-only messages, anything that smells bulk |
| What causes engagement | A reel forwarded with a one-line personal endorsement; a poll or question in a broker group |
| What causes a demo | A 1:1 chat that answers a pricing or objection question in *their* language within minutes |

**Implication for production:** Instagram content must be **forward-optimised** — legible as a thumbnail in a WhatsApp chat list, self-explanatory without the caption, and emotionally shareable into a broker group.

## 6. Facebook groups — trust seeding

Groups such as "Mumbai Real Estate Agents" are roughly 20% of consumption (estimate). Tone is raw and peer-to-peer.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What gets attention | Raw text confessions, questions seeking peer advice, commiseration posts |
| What gets ignored | Polished branded creative (read as an ad), overt self-promo, anything corporate |
| What causes engagement | Vulnerability plus a question; "kisi ne ye face kiya?"; mini-polls; controversy |
| What causes a DM | A genuinely helpful comment on someone's pain post. **Value first, never pitch first.** |

In groups, RealEstateFlow shows up as a helpful peer voice in **text** — the founder posting under their own name is fine here; the founder on camera is not. Native text beats branded video. This is a soft-attention surface, not a direct-response one.

## 7. LinkedIn — the credibility mirror

Roughly 5% of consumption, used mostly for recruiting agents rather than lead generation. Its job is to make an owner feel safe choosing a "real" product.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What gets attention | Owner-flavoured authority: "Why brokerages lose leads to follow-up gaps (and how to measure yours)"; RERA changes; build-in-public engineering notes |
| What gets ignored | Hinglish memes, drama skits (wrong register), pure entertainment |
| What causes engagement | Numbered insights, contrarian B2B takes |
| What causes a demo | A "serious, modern product" signal that de-risks the buyer's reputation |

English-leaning only. Carries authority and founder-text formats, never the reach engine. **Named case studies are not available to us** — the June draft's "How a 15-agent Pune brokerage cut lead leakage to near-zero" is fabricated and must not be revived.

Five drafted founder posts already exist at `marketing-and-sales/launch-plan-v2/linkedin-posts/`.

## 8. The moment goldmine — pick a moment for a goal

Emotionally charged moments from broker life, scored by the attention action each is best at causing.

| # | Moment | Best outcome | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lead lost in the WhatsApp scroll | **Share + DM** | Universal pain, cathartic, "tag a broker" |
| 2 | Client asks "any update?" for the third time | **Save + DM** | Embarrassment converts to "I need a fix" |
| 3 | Two agents claim the same lead | **Share** | Office-war relatability |
| 4 | An agent quits and takes the leads | **DM** | High-stakes loss aversion for the owner |
| 5 | Commission dispute | **Save + demo** | Khata pain leads straight into a product reveal |
| 6 | A competitor closed *your* deal, faster | **DM** | Status threat plus a speed angle |
| 7 | A spreadsheet error costs real money | **Save** | "Could be me", plus a fix |
| 10 | A competitor's polished WhatsApp status | **Save + DM** | Status and inadequacy convert to "I want that" |
| 13 | A paid lead called back the next day, already gone | **Demo** | Speed of response is the whole argument |
| 14 | The son who is uninterested until he sees the system | **Share** | Succession warmth |

Selection rule: **want shares → 1, 3, 14. Want saves → 2, 5, 7. Want DMs and demos → 4, 6, 13.**

## 9. Attention timing

| Window | State | Best energy |
|---|---|---|
| 6–8 AM (coffee scroll) | Fresh, doom-scrolling WhatsApp and IG | Pain recognition, validation |
| 12–1 PM (lunch) | Quick dopamine, gossip | Memes, contrarian takes, quick wins |
| 6–8 PM (wind-down) | Reflective, planning tomorrow | Aspiration, product proof, demo asks |
| 9–11 PM | Exhausted, low intent | Light formats, binge rewatch |
| Friday evening | Relaxed, high engagement | Best reach bets |
| Monday morning | Work mode, low scroll | Avoid heavy launches |

Mumbai's 6–8 AM WhatsApp window is the single richest. The month-1 pack's own posting rule (9:30 PM) was set later and against real platform behaviour; where the two disagree, the month-1 pack wins for M1 scheduling.

## 11. Quick reference

- **Attention is granted to:** a number they calculate themselves, a mirror of their own pain, WhatsApp chaos, a real product screen.
- **Attention is denied to:** jargon, stock photos, logo intros, "transformation", slow builds.
- **Saves** come from useful. **Shares** come from funny and relatable. **DMs** come from keyword loops and honest product magic.
- **WhatsApp is the forwarding layer · Facebook groups seed trust · LinkedIn reassures the buyer · Instagram is the engine.**
- One persona, one attention state, one framework per piece.
- No customers means no peer proof. Product proof is what we have, and it is enough.
